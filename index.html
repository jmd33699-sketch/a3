Kinetic Architecture

In-House Calibre Architecture: Cycloidal Gear Geometries

The beating heart of every Mechanical Hour timepiece is an entirely proprietary in-house calibre. Rather than modifying commercial ebauches, our horological engineers design the wheel train from ground zero.

Our gear teeth are cut using mathematically optimized cycloidal and involute profiles, polished to an Ra roughness under 0.05 microns. This micro-geometric precision reduces frictional power loss across the five wheels of the gear train by 28%, allowing our mainspring to deliver a flat torque curve across its full 72-hour power reserve.

  • ✓ Maillechort German Silver Bridges: Unplated nickel-silver alloy with rich golden patina.
  • ✓ Solid Gold Chatons: Friction-free synthetic ruby jewels set in 18K gold screwed cups.
  • ✓ Twin Serial Barrels: Equalized torque delivery preventing spring recoil isochronal drift.
Chronometric Heart

The Free-Sprung Gyromax Balance & Breguet Terminal Curve

In standard mass-produced mechanical watches, timing regulation is achieved through an index regulator (raquette) that changes the effective vibrating length of the hairspring. While easy to adjust, an index regulator introduces isochronal errors whenever the watch is bumped or flexed.

Mechanical Hour utilizes solely free-sprung variable-inertia balances. The hairspring breathes freely without interfering regulator pins. Rate adjustments are made directly on the rim of the balance wheel by rotating four opposing gold inertia weights (Gyromax collets). Combined with a hand-formed Breguet overcoil terminal curve, the balance expands concentric to its center of gravity in all orientations, achieving COSC chronometer certification with ease.

Art of Decoration

Hand-Polished Anglage, Black Mirror Luster & Côtes de Genève

More than half the total creation time of our timepieces is devoted strictly to movement decoration. In horology, hand finishing is not merely decorative; it removes microscopic machining burrs that could shed metal dust into the delicate ruby pivots.

Every steel and German silver bridge undergoes hand anglage (beveling). Using wooden pegwood sticks dipped in diamond micro-paste, the artisan transforms sharp 90-degree CNC edges into flawless, 45-degree mirror-polished chamfers with crisp interior angles. The screw heads and tourbillon steel bridges are black-polished (poli noir) on tin zinc laps until they absorb all ambient light, reflecting pure black from one angle and blinding white light from another.

Hand-Drawn Côtes de Genève Waves

Traditional boxwood laps create rhythmic, parallel Geneva wave striations across bridges that dance beneath changing light.
